Sovran Advisor on your students' phones

A phone web app. Students open one link, tap “Add to Home Screen”, and get the crown icon like any other app. It works on iPhone and Android, with no App Store. Their computer app stays the main home of their data. The phone is a light companion that reads from your Sovran Library and sends things back to the computer.

3 · The Assistant on the phone

The hard part, in plain words

On the computer, the Assistant is the student's own Claude (or ChatGPT), signed in with their own plan. It reads their journal, notes and people right there on the computer. A phone can't run that. So the phone has three ways to ask.

A · Ask, and the computer answers My pick to start

The phone sends the question through the drop box. The computer's Claude answers with everything it knows, and the answer comes back to the phone.

  • Costs you nothing. Uses the student's own Claude plan.
  • Best answers: it sees all their data and the Library.
  • Private: the question is sealed, like phone capture.
  • Only answers while their computer is on and the app is running. Otherwise the answer waits.
B · A cloud AI you pay for

A small helper in your cloud calls Claude directly with your API key, and answers in seconds, any time.

  • Instant, works when the computer is off.
  • You pay per question: about 2 to 3 cents on Sonnet, well under 1 cent on Haiku. A busy student might cost $1 to $5 a month.
  • It only sees the Library and the phone copy, not their whole computer.
  • Needs limits per student so costs can't run away.
C · No Assistant on the phone

The phone reads, writes and captures. Questions wait for the computer.

  • Simplest. Saves about a week of work.
  • Feels less alive. Students will ask for it.

A can grow into B later. If students love it, you could switch on B as a paid extra, without rebuilding anything.

4 · How we'd build it

Six steps, each one usable on its own

Each step ends with something you can try on your own phone. We can pause after any step. Effort is Claude's building time. “You” is what each step needs from you.

  1. 1

    The app shell and pairing

    A phone page with the crown icon, the VIG look and five tabs. In the computer app, Settings › Phone shows a QR code. The student scans it once, and the phone is paired with its own phone key. “New link” cuts off a lost phone.

    You: try it on your iPhone, then on an Android phone.
    3–4 days
  2. 2

    Reports and teaching on the phone

    A new read-only doorway in your Library cloud. The phone shows the student's own reports (like the Reports page) and the teaching notes. Reports keep their Ivory & Gold look.

    You: approve one cloud change (the 60-second approval box). Read your own Pattern on your phone.
    3–4 days
  3. 3

    Write: journal lines and capture

    Today's journal, a quick note and a task go through the drop box we already have. The computer puts each one in the right place: Journal, Inbox or Tasks. Dictation is the phone keyboard's microphone, tidied by their own Claude like now.

    You: write a journal line on your phone and see it land on your computer.
    2–3 days
  4. 4
    Optional, recommended

    The phone copy: see today on the phone

    While the computer app runs, it sends a small sealed copy every few minutes: today's plan, open tasks, the last 30 days of journal, and people's names. Only the phone and computer hold the key to open it. Your cloud only holds locked boxes.

    You: approve one cloud change. Check that today's plan shows on the phone.
    1–1½ weeks
  5. 5

    The Assistant (option A)

    Ask on the phone; the computer answers with the student's own Claude and sends the answer back, sealed. The phone shows “Waiting for your computer” when it's off.

    You: ask your phone a question about your Pattern.
    about 1 week
  6. 6

    Test with real students, then launch

    Two or three students try it on their own phones for a week. Then a new door on the Start Here page (“Put Sovran Advisor on your phone”), a help page, a short film, and the student app update.

    You: pick the testers, say “merge it”, and post the door to WhatsApp.
    about 1 week

Total: about 4 to 6 weeks from your go, depending on your testing time. Steps 1 to 3 alone (about 2 weeks) already give students reports, teaching, journal and capture on the phone.

6 · Honest trade-offs

What it won't do, and what to watch

The computer is still the boss

Everything written on the phone lands on the computer. If the computer is off for a week, the phone copy is a week old, and new phone writing waits in the drop box (up to 90 days) until the computer collects it.

No editing old things on the phone

The phone adds new lines, notes and tasks. Changing an old note on the phone needs full two-way sync (the “Everywhere” bundle on your Later list). That's a much bigger job with real data-loss risks.

Not in the App Store

Some students will look for it in the store. The Start Here door and a short film fix that. On iPhone it must be added from Safari.

Phone limits

iPhone web apps can show notifications only after being added to the home screen, and can't run in the background. The phone may clear its saved copy if the app isn't opened for weeks; nothing is lost, it fetches again.

More in your cloud

With step 4, your cloud holds sealed copies of students' journals. You can't read them, but it needs a short privacy line for students. That line is already on your Later list.

Windows students too

The phone doesn't care whether the computer is a Mac or a PC. Windows computers are still less tested overall.

7 · Your choices

🔴 Four things for you to decide

🔴 1. The Assistant on the phone: A, B or C?

My pick: A. No cost to you, best answers, and B can be added later.

🔴 2. Include step 4, the phone copy of today?

My pick: yes. Without it the phone can write but can't show their plan, tasks or journal, and feels half there.

🔴 3. Which address?

My pick: your own, like app.livesovran.com, if you own the domain. Otherwise the free github.io link works fine.

🔴 4. Who gets it first?

My pick: you, then 2 or 3 students for a week, then everyone with the next update.
