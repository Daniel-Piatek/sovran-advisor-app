# AGENTS.md

How the AI agent (Claude Code or any other coding agent) works on this project. Read it at the start of every session. It is a starting point: add to it and change it as the project grows.

## Sovran Advisor

- This is **Sovran Advisor**, the app Sovran students run on their own computer. It started as their mentor's own app and is updated from the Sovran Advisor repository on GitHub (see `INSTALL.md` for installing and updating).
- The owner's data lives in `data/` only (the database, their files, `data/backups/`). Never edit, move or delete anything in `data/`, and never commit it.
- To make it their own, the owner changes settings, switches sections on in Settings › Modules, or adds modules of their own. Changing the files that came with the app works too, but the next update may clash with those changes: say so before making one. `CUSTOMISING.md` is the owner's own guide to this.
- **`MY-CHANGES.md` is the owner's list of their own changes** (left out of git, so no update ever touches it). Read it at the start of every session. After every change to their copy, add a short entry: the date, what they asked for in their words, the files touched, and why. Write the owner's own rules, lessons learned and details of their computer and phone there too, not in this file: this file comes with the app, and an update replaces it. Create `MY-CHANGES.md` the first time it is needed.
- Prefer new files of their own (a new module, a new screen) over editing the app's files, so updates combine cleanly. Commit each finished change in their copy (it is never pushed anywhere), so an update can merge it.
- **Voice calls** (2026-11-12, myICOR advanced/voice/3-7, part of the `voice` module, schema 70): `call.js` (server: settings, speaking, warming, plain questions answered from your data with no model, turns and the first-word numbers, the Voice log), `public/call.js` (the Call button and widget over the chat: turn taking, barge-in, fillers, read-out cards with a spoken yes, Go on in writing), `public/spoken.js` (what is said aloud), `public/voice-settings.js` (Settings › Voice and Voice log). Without an ElevenLabs key it listens with Whisper and speaks with Piper "Alba" (en_GB, the owner's pick) through `sherpa-onnx-node`, pinned to 1.13.8 because 2.x dropped espeak and skips names and numbers; both models fetched once into data/voice-model/. With a key, ElevenLabs for both. Tests stand in for the ears (`window.__sovranCallEar`) and the voice (`VOICE_FAKE_SPEECH`).
- Mail is part of Sovran Advisor but dormant: the module `mail` is off in a new install (`starter: false`), holds no Google credentials, and its jobs (Live with Gmail, the sender) do nothing while it is off; directions for setting up a Google sign-in come later. Google Calendar and Dictation are switched off too until the owner switches them on (Google Calendar needs their own Google account; Dictation uses the free voice model on their computer, voice-local.js, fetched once into data/voice-model/, or their own ElevenLabs key when one is pasted in Settings). Habits (`mylife-plus`) are off until switched on. **Phone, backups and moving** (`everywhere`, move.js, backup.js checks, public/quick.html, public/everywhere-settings.js) is off until switched on: the phone Capture page /quick, each daily backup checked, and a moving file (a .tar.gz of app.db and data/files) brought in only after the owner's yes, at the next start, with what was there kept in data/before-move-<time>/.
- Schema numbers differ from APP Daniel's from 67 on: here 67 is the AI choice (`chat_messages.ai`, `model`), 68 Habits, 69 Mail drafts, 70 voice calls (`call_turns`, `voice_log`) (APP Daniel: 67 Habits, 68 drafts, 69 the AI choice). Never renumber a step that has shipped.

## Design rules

Every agent follows these on every screen. The look lives in `public/tokens.css`; read it before building anything you can see.

- **One accent colour, on one thing per screen.** The accent is `--color-accent`, the brand's Crimson (`#c71f42` light, a lighter `#ea5575` dark; 2026-11-08, from the Live Sovran brand handoff v3, replacing the blue). Use it on exactly one thing per screen: the action that matters most there. Everything else stays black, white and grey. A screen with no main action has no accent at all.
- **Each kind has its colour** (2026-11-09, the owner's wish, from the Live Sovran palette): Notes Cobalt, Journal Yellow (2026-11-10, swapped with the Assistant), Tasks Orange, Calendar Turquoise, the Planner a lighter Turquoise (2026-11-09), People Violet, the Timeline Charcoal (2026-11-09; a light stone in the dark theme), My Life Green (its Goals Gold, Projects Orange, Topics Cobalt: 2026-11-09), the Assistant Gold (2026-11-10) (`--kind-*` in `tokens.css`). The colour is a disc behind the kind's icon wherever that icon shows, a ring on a person's face, and a thin line on top of that section's screen; never a fill behind words. ## How we work

- **Plan first.** Propose small steps, each ending in something the owner can see and check. Wait for a go.
- **One feature at a time.** Finish, check and commit it before starting the next one.
- **Find the cause before fixing.** Add a test that would have caught the problem.
- **Don't break what works.** Change existing code only when needed, and say what changed and why.
- **Keep it simple.** Choose the simplest solution the owner can understand. Add no dependency without a reason.
- **Ask about the owner's decisions:** looks, wording, what data is kept.

## Big picture first

- Build the big parts of the app first, simply, so the whole idea works end to end. Polish comes after that.
- When the owner goes deep into the details of one screen while the core is not built yet, say so briefly. Suggest noting the detail for later. The choice is theirs.

## Bring over what worked

- If the owner has built something similar before (a folder or a repository), ask once where it is.
- Read it, say what worked and what did not, and bring over the good parts instead of starting from scratch.

## Done means checked

Before handing work back, check it yourself:

- **The checks pass:** the tests, and types and build if the stack has them.
- **It ran for real.** Run the app with test data, never real data. Use the feature in a browser, on a large and a small screen, with no errors in the console.
- **An end-to-end test covers the main path.** Each test starts its own copy of the app with an empty database, and none depends on a real AI model.
- **Nothing the owner enters is lost.** Deleting can be undone.
- **README.md says what works now,** how to run it and why it works this way, in plain words, updated in the same commit.

## Reporting back

End with a short report:
- what now works
- how it was tested
- what is left, risky or not verified
- what the owner needs to decide

Never call something done without having checked it.

## Safety

- Never print, log or commit secrets or personal data.
- Never weaken a test to make it pass.
- Ask before anything that cannot be undone: pushing, deploying, deleting data, changing a live database.

## Git

Commit after every finished step, with a plain message that says what changed. Once there is a remote, push often so it is easy to roll back; ask before the first push.

## Lessons learned

When something goes wrong, add a short rule with the reason, so it does not happen again (in the owner's copy, in `MY-CHANGES.md`).

- **To prove a check fails, undo only the change you made on purpose.** Never `git checkout` a file to undo it: that also throws away the work not yet committed in that file. (2026-10-25: it wiped the new `style.css` once; it was rewritten from the session.)
- **Test a drop the way a person drops.** Dropping a file only worked when it was let go exactly over a line of text; the test had dropped it right on the first line. Tests now drag (dragenter, dragover, drop) onto the empty space, the title and other parts of the page (`dragFileTo` in `tests/e2e/attachments.spec.js`).
- **Look at the screens, not only the tests.** A stray "null" sat under the notes list with every test passing; a screenshot found it. There is now a test for stray "null"/"undefined" on every screen, but still look before reporting.
- **Check that a hint or style really shows, not only that it is set.** The editor's "Start writing" hint was set on every empty editor for days but never drawn, because `attr()` read the wrong element; tests checked the attribute, not the drawn text. Test what is drawn (for example `getComputedStyle(el, '::before').content`).
- **`replaceChildren` and `append` draw `null` as the word "null"; only `el()` skips it.** The add bar's compact Home chip showed a stray "null" (2026-10-25). Spread a list instead: `...(cond ? [x] : [])`.
- **A menu that opens beside something must stay on the screen.** The home picker opened past the phone's right edge in the Tasks app's edit strip and could not be tapped (2026-10-25); it now moves left and scrolls into view. Test menus on the phone where they open near an edge.
- **A day is not always 24 hours.** Moving an event "a day" by adding 1440 minutes put it an hour early on 25 October 2026, when the clocks went back in Athens. Move dates with `setDate` (it keeps the clock time) and minutes separately; there is a test for that night.
- **Only the latest load draws.** Two quick changes on the quarter page sent two reloads, and the older answer, arriving last, drew over the newer one: a Goal just added seemed gone (2026-10-26, a test that failed now and then). The Planner's `load` draws only its latest request.
- **Quick presses must queue.** Two moves sent before the first answer came back both started from the old time. Changes that build on the last one wait their turn (the Calendar's `move`).
- **A redraw must keep what is being typed.** Marking a Goal in the quarter redrew the page when the server answered and wiped the quarter's line being typed (2026-10-26, found by a test that failed once in a full run). A box that can be redrawn keeps its draft (`drafts` in `public/quarter.js`) and, if it had the cursor, gets it back at the end of the words: without that, the next letter typed was lost (2026-10-26, "Steady" came out "Stead"). A test types while a save is under way.
- **Give the cursor back in the same moment as the redraw, not a frame later.** The quarter's line lost letters ("Steady" became "Stea") when a redraw put the cursor back a frame later: keys pressed in between went nowhere (2026-10-26, found when the computer was busy). Draw with `wantsCursor(box)` and call `giveCursorBack(place)` right after putting it on the page (`public/ui.js`). And a test that waits for a redraw must wait for a sign that really changes: a closed day's counts don't.
- **On a phone, End doesn't move the cursor once the page can scroll.** The Ask your notes box made short pages a little taller than a phone screen, and four tests that pressed End typed at the start (2026-10-26). A phone has no End key: tests put the cursor at the end the way a person does, with `tapAtEnd` in `tests/e2e/fixtures.js`.
- **Test the widths between a phone and a large screen too.** At 800 px the Planner kept the Tray beside the day and squeezed a task's words to one letter a line, with every test passing: tests ran only at 1280 and on a phone (2026-10-26, found by the owner's own window). A row's words now keep a minimum width and its chips wrap; a test checks every width from 700 to 1440.
- **Commit only on the tests' own exit code.** `npm test | grep …` succeeds whatever the tests did, and once let a commit through with two failing tests (2026-10-25). Run the tests, keep their exit code, and commit only when it is 0.
- **Test typing at a person's pace around the editor.** The editor learns where the cursor is a moment after an arrow key or End; a test that presses the next key at once types in the wrong place. Use the `settle` helper in `tests/e2e/editor.spec.js` after moving the cursor. Tools that paste text in one go (not key by key) don't trigger the editor's `**`/`#` rules, so check formatting with real key presses.
